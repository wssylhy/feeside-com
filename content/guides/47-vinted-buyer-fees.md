# Vinted Buyer Fees Explained

Intro: Vinted's famous "no fees" applies to sellers. Buyers pay the platform instead: a buyer protection fee added at checkout on top of the item price and the shipping label. That fee funds buyer protection and payment handling, and it is the reason the seller side can stay at `{{rates.vinted.final_value_percent}}%` across every listing. *(渲染自 config；买家侧费率结构出自官方说明，具体金额因地区而异)*

## What the buyer sees at checkout

Three lines: the item price (which reaches the seller in full), the shipping label, and the Buyer Protection fee — a percentage of the item price plus a small fixed amount, sized by region. The seller never sees or touches those last two lines. This is the structural opposite of Poshmark, where the platform's revenue comes out of a seller-side commission and the buyer's checkout is simpler.

## Why Vinted can afford zero seller fees

The protection fee shifts the platform's revenue to whoever is already paying for something (the buyer), instead of taking a cut from whoever is getting paid (the seller). For sellers that means the listing price is the payout, which is why Vinted tops every cheapest-platform ranking at every price point. For buyers it means the advertised item price is never the final price.

## What this means when comparing platforms

If you are choosing where to sell, the buyer fee is mostly a conversion detail: buyers compare final prices, and a higher checkout total can cost you the sale on price-sensitive items. If you are choosing where to buy, the item price is only part of what you will pay — add the protection fee and the label before comparing with other marketplaces.

## FAQ

**Does Vinted charge buyers every time?**
Yes, on standard purchases: the protection fee is added to every checkout alongside the shipping label.

**How big is the buyer protection fee?**
A percentage of the item price plus a small fixed amount; the exact numbers vary by region and are shown to the buyer at checkout before payment.

**Do sellers ever pay the protection fee?**
No. It never touches the seller's payout — that is exactly why the seller side stays at zero.
